Radio Life Writing

A non-exhaustive collection of German auto/biographical radio programmes analysed on the basis of archival metadata and semantic models.

According to the definition by The Oxford Centre for Life-Writing, life writing ‘includes every possible way of telling a life-story, from biography and autobiography, through letters and memoir, to bio-fiction, blogs, and social media such as Tweets and Instagram stories’. This project focused on life writing on German radio: autobiographical and biographical radio dramas, features, documentaries and portraits based on audio material, among others.

Within the framework of this project, disparate metadata from digitalised German radio archives is being converted into a corpus for the study of radio life writing in order to track whose lives were featured during which time periods, which sub-genres of radio programmes are popular for covering these lives, which narrative modes were used to tell these life stories, and the length of these programmes.

This project does not constitute a definitive canon of German radio life writing. Nevertheless, it may serve as a guide for, for example:

  • finding likely examples of German radio life writing;
  • seeing how radio life writing appears across forms, genres and years;
  • comparing DRA catalogue records with DLF/Wirklichkeit programme pages;
  • identifying clusters such as diaries, letters, portraits, autobiography, social documentary lives, and sound-art/listening cases;
  • choosing promising cases for close listening and archival checking.

It is based on 1,764 aggregated German records, and displays a selection map compiled following manual verification and the exclusion of irrelevant data.

*For details of how this was done, see selection_method.ipynb.

Corpus triage

Who are these lives about?

For each selected entry, the biographical subject was identified, and preliminary tags reflecting the main theme of their life were then added. A single entry may contain several tags.
