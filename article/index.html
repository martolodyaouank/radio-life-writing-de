Lives on Air

A German-first corpus of biographical radio drama, documentary feature, and sound art — explored through archive metadata and latent semantic representations.

This project turns scattered German radio archive and programme metadata into a corpus for studying biographical radio drama, documentary feature, and sound-based life writing.

It is not a final canon; it is a navigational layer for finding patterns, absences, and promising cases for close listening. The pipeline combines DRA / ARD Hoerspieldatenbank, Hoerspiel und Feature, and Wirklichkeit im Radio, then models the high- and medium-confidence subset with TF-IDF, truncated SVD embeddings, k-means clusters, and t-SNE projection — tuned to acoustic memory: voice, sound material, archives, testimony, family history, and the institutional forms through which radio remembers.

Corpus triage

DRA supplies the historical backbone; Hoerspiel und Feature adds recent programme pages; Wirklichkeit im Radio is small but unusually rich in critical commentary. Confidence levels mark discovery quality, not scholarly certainty.

Who are these lives about?

For each curated record we extract whom it appears to be about, then add provisional life-focus tags. A record can carry several — the subject line is the anchor, the tags are aids for sorting.

Cluster terms

Clusters are labelled from their strongest terms, then checked against the nearest exemplar works. They differ by acoustic-memory makeup, not only by title keywords.

Source mix

Some clusters are archive-led; others are shaped by DLF web descriptions or Wirklichkeit essays.

Historical Form

Biography is distributed across adaptation, feature, portrait, original radio play, and sound art.

Cluster profiles

Share of core records flagged for each memory signal. Trauma and family dominate; voice, sound material and explicit archive references are the rarer, more deliberate moves.

How long do lives run?

Median running time by cluster (minutes), with the typical range. Duration is a rough proxy, but it separates compact portrait and catalogue pieces from the longer documentary and autobiographical forms that invite extended memory work.

What is useful here

01

The corpus is now searchable as a German radio-memory field, not just a list of scraped pages.

02

The latent clusters create a close-reading shortlist: diaries, letters, portraits, sound art, social documentary, and curated criticism.

03

The method exposes where metadata is thin — DRA is historically broad, while Wirklichkeit and DLF are text-rich enough for semantic modelling.

04

The next serious step is to replace TF-IDF / SVD with sentence-transformer embeddings and manually review cluster exemplars.
